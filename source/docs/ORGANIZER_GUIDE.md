# Organiser's guide

How to run an event with this system, start to finish. The planning figures
come from the ARA's [Tournament-in-a-Box](https://arariichi.org/tournamentinabox)
guide; the scoring follows [WRC 2025](https://www.worldriichi.org/wrc-rules).

---

## Before the day

### Equipment, per four players

The ARA's checklist, which this system does not change:

| Item | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Mahjong set, mat, compass | 1 each per table (+1 spare) |
| Table | 1 per 4 players, about 6 ft × 6 ft of space |
| Chairs | 4–6 per table |
| Report sheet | 1 per table per round |
| Points reference sheet | 1 per table (+10%) |
| Pens | 1 per 2 players |
| Water | 2 bottles per player per day (+20%) |
| Coffee | 1 carafe per 12 players |
| First aid kit | 1 |

The ARA costed this at roughly **$101.50 per person** in 2024 buying materials,
or **$78.50 per person** renting tables and chairs. Allow two months' lead time,
or six-plus months if you need a hotel. Hotels size rooms at about 10 ft × 10 ft
per table.

### Staffing

The ARA suggests **1 tournament organiser, 1 judge per 8 tables, 1 runner**,
plus **3 sit-in players** to fill tables when the field is uneven.

Add your sit-ins to the roster with the role **Sit-in**. A sit-in is seated only
when there are enough of them to complete the last table; when the ranked players
fill their tables on their own, the sit-ins sit out. They never take a seat from a
ranked player, and they never appear in the standings. When a round needs fewer
sit-ins than you have, the ones who have played least go in first.

### Set the event up in advance

Create the tournament and paste the roster the night before. Then on the day you
only have to sign people in and draw the first round.

Run it on a laptop you can leave open for the day. One machine runs the whole
event — there is nothing to connect to and nothing to log in to.

### Choose your backup folder first

This is the one setup step that matters. Open **Backups & print** and pick a
folder before the day starts.

While you work, your results are held inside the browser, and that storage
belongs to the browser on this computer — **copying the HTML file somewhere else
does not take the tournament with it.** The backup folder is what makes the event
portable and what you recover from.

Choose a folder inside **OneDrive, Google Drive or Dropbox** and every round is
copied off the laptop automatically, with no account to set up. If the machine
dies at lunchtime, the morning is already safe on another computer.

Picking a folder works in Chrome and Edge. In Safari or Firefox the app asks you
to save a backup file by hand after each round, and warns you when one is
overdue — workable, but the Chrome path is the one to prefer for a real event.

## A sample day

The ARA's four-round schedule, for reference:

| Time | |
|---|---|
| 08:30–09:15 | Sign-in |
| 09:15–09:30 | Announcements |
| 09:30 | Hanchan 1 |
| 11:30 | Hanchan 2 |
| 13:00 | Lunch |
| 14:30 | Hanchan 3 |
| 16:30 | Hanchan 4 |
| 18:00 | Free play |

Hanchan are **90 minutes** with **no extra hands** once time is called; allow
about 15 minutes to play out and report and 15 minutes of break, so roughly 30
minutes between rounds. Call the time remaining at 30, 45, 60 and 75 minutes.

## On the day

### 1. Sign-in

Sign people in on the Players tab as they arrive. Tick **Here** beside their
name, or type part of it in the find box and press Enter once only one name
matches; the box is ready for the next person straight away. The count above
the roster shows how many of the field are in.

When everyone who is coming has arrived, **Withdraw the ones not signed in**
marks the rest withdrawn in one go, after showing you the names, so they are
left out of the draw. Withdraw rather than delete: deleting loses a player's
history and is blocked once they have played. On a second day, **Clear
sign-in** starts again.

Prefer paper at the door? Print the sign-in sheet from **Backups & print** and
tick people off on the laptop afterwards.

Check the player count on the Players tab before you draw. It tells you directly
how the field divides, for example:

> 63 ranked · **15 tables** · **3 sitting out each round**
> (add 1 more player, or 1 more sit-in, to fill the last table)

### 2. Draw the seating

**Run the round → Draw the seating.** It takes a second or two and reports what
it found:

> Hanchan 1 seated: 15 tables, no repeat pairings.

The round header shows repeat pairings and the worst seat-wind gap for any
player, so you can see the draw's quality at a glance. If you do not like it and
nothing has been entered yet, **Redraw seating** draws again.

For a very large field in a hurry, the **ARA wheel** option is instant, though
the optimised draw is better and still only takes a second or two.

Byes rotate automatically: nobody sits out twice before everybody has sat out
once.

### Changing seats after the draw

**Change seats**, on the round page, moves people without redrawing:

- Two seated players swap seats.
- Someone sitting out takes a seat, and the player they replace sits out on the
  same bye score. This is how you make a particular person sit out a round.
- Someone not in this round takes a seat: a late arrival, or a sit-in covering a
  no-show. The player they replace leaves the round with no score. If your rules
  penalise a missed hanchan, add the penalty on the Cut & finals tab.

Nobody else at the table moves. A player at a table that already has a score
stays where they are. Reprint the seating and that table's report sheet if they
are already out.

### 3. Print and lay out

- **Print seating** — a list by table and a list by player. Put the by-player
  list where people queue.
- **Print report sheets** — one per table, with the seat winds, the riichi-stick
  row, the required total, and signature lines for the players and the referee.
- **Print table numbers** once at the start of the event.

### The clock and the room display

The round page has a **Clock** for the round, set from *Minutes per hanchan* on
the Setup tab. Press **Start the clock** when play begins; **Pause** and
**Resume** if play stops, and nothing is lost. It turns amber with fifteen
minutes left, and at zero says the hand in play finishes and no new one starts.
Closing the round stops it.

**Open the room display** for a second window to put on a projector or a big
screen: drag it there and make it full screen. It shows the clock with one of:

- **Seating** — everyone in the round alphabetically, with their table and
  wind, so players find themselves without crowding the laptop;
- **Standings** — the event's standings, with a line at the cut;
- **Clock only** — the clock, as large as it will go.

Choose which from the round page. The display keeps itself up to date as you
work, and needs this window to stay open.

### 4. Play and report

Players fill in the paper sheet and a referee signs it, as WRC requires. The
runner then types it in.

**Tap a table.** Type the four final scores. As you type:

- the running total is shown against 120,000 and turns green when it is right;
- with three scores entered, there is a one-tap button to fill in the fourth;
- once all four are in you see the placements, each player's delta, uma and
  hanchan score, before saving.

Enter any **riichi sticks left on the table** in the field provided — the
checksum accounts for them, and they are carried into the standings exactly as
the rules require.

A table whose scores do not add up cannot be saved by mistake; the message says
how far out it is. If the signed sheet genuinely says that, and you need to record
it as it is, **Record as signed** appears under the preview once all four scores
are in. It asks you to confirm, the table is marked *Recorded as signed* from
then on, and the log keeps the entry.

Chombo and penalties are under **Chombo and penalties** on the same sheet. A
chombo takes 30P off that player's tournament score and does not change the
table total — this is the WRC treatment, and there is no mangan payment.

Mark a table **verified** once a judge has checked the sheet. Tables show as
*to play*, *entered* or *verified*, so you can see at a glance what is
outstanding.

### 5. Close the round

**Close this round.** If a table has no result the button says so and asks you
to confirm. Closing locks the scores, takes a snapshot, and lets you draw the
next hanchan.

To fix a score after closing, press **Correct** beside the round in the round
list and tap the table. It works even after later rounds are seated: the round
stays closed and nobody's seat changes. Say why on the sheet; the log keeps the
scores as they were alongside the new ones, and the table is marked as corrected.
Once the cut is made, qualifying tables can no longer be corrected this way,
because a change could alter who should have gone through. Undo the cut first,
which is possible until a final is seated.

**Reopen** is still there for anything else, as long as no later round has been
seated.

## Making the cut

When the qualifying rounds are done, go to **Cut & finals**, set the size and
how many final hanchan, and choose what happens to the points:

- **Reset to zero** — the ARA format: the top 4 start the finals level.
- **Carry the full score** — qualifying scores carry into the finals.
- **Carry half** — a middle ground.

Only players still in the event go through. Anyone marked **Withdrawn** keeps
their qualifying place but is passed over, and the next player goes through in
their seat. The Cut & finals tab names whoever was passed over.

The first final round seats by rank (a single table takes ranks 1–4; with more
than one table the seeds are spread). Later final rounds are re-paired so the
same four do not sit together every time.

Standings then has three views: **Qualifying**, **Finals**, and **Overall**.

After the cut, **Overall** is how the event finished: the finalists in their
finals order, then everyone else by their qualifying score, placed after them.
Nobody who missed the cut can rank above a finalist, and under a reset the
winner of the final is first however the qualifying went. Until a final hanchan
has been scored, the finalists stand in their seeding order. `standings.csv`,
the printed standings and the Discord post all follow this view, and the CSV's
`stage` column says which score each row is.

### If the cut line is tied

The cut will refuse and tell you who is level:

> The cut line is tied: Gil, Hal share rank 4. Settle it (a coin toss, per WRC)
> by setting a manual tiebreak on the roster, then cut again.

WRC's tiebreakers run: score, then most firsts, then fewest fourths, then best
single hanchan, then a coin toss. The first four are automatic. For the coin
toss, give the winner a higher **Tiebreak** number on the Players tab — any
distinct numbers work, higher ranks higher — and make the cut again. Players
still level are marked with an asterisk in the standings, so you always know
when a decision is owed.

### Free play for everyone else

Each final you draw also seats everyone who missed the cut at **free-play
tables**, numbered after the final tables. They appear on the round page, the
printed seating and the Discord post like any table, marked *free play*, but
they have no report sheet and no score entry, they never count towards the
standings, and closing the final does not wait for them. If the numbers do not
divide into fours, whoever is left over is listed on the round page.

The box for this is on the Cut & finals tab, ticked by default. Untick it to
seat the finalists only; a change after the cut applies from the next final.

Between rounds, or after the finals, you can also draw a whole round with the
phase set to **Free play**: everyone is seated, nobody is scored.

## Posting to Discord

The app can keep one pairings post and one standings post up to date in a
Discord channel, so players can check their table from their phones. It is off
until you set it up, and it is the only thing in the app that sends anything
over the internet.

1. In Discord, open the channel's settings: **Integrations → Webhooks → New
   Webhook**, then **Copy Webhook URL**.
2. In the app, paste it under **Post to Discord** on the Backups & print tab
   and press **Save**.

From then on the posts update by themselves: the pairings when a round is drawn
or seats change, the Overall standings as tables are entered and rounds close. Each
update edits the existing post rather than adding a new message. **Post now**
sends both straight away.

Worth knowing:

- **Players' names leave the computer** when you post. That is the point, but
  make sure your players are happy with it.
- **The webhook URL is a password** for that channel. The app keeps it in this
  browser only, out of your backup folder and out of restore points, so a new
  laptop needs it pasted again.
- **A webhook can post and nothing else.** It cannot read messages or see your
  members.
- **Test tournaments post too**, coloured and labelled as a test, so you can
  rehearse. Point the webhook at a channel where made-up names are welcome.

## Backups

You do not need to do anything for backups to happen. After every change the
system rewrites a full set of CSVs and appends to a log that is never rewritten.
Each time a round closes it also takes a dated snapshot.

Worth knowing:

- **`live/` is safe to open mid-event.** Files are written to a temporary name
  and renamed, so you will never read a half-written file.
- **`log/results_log.csv` is the real safety net.** Every score ever entered is
  appended there, corrections included. Even if everything else were lost, the
  event could be rebuilt from it.
- **`live/results.csv` has a `table_total_check` column** on every row, so the
  arithmetic can be checked outside this program.
- **`live/ara_sheet.csv`** is one row per player and one column per round — the
  ARA spreadsheet layout, for dropping into the sheet you already use.

**Take a snapshot now** before anything risky — reopening a closed round, say,
or changing a scoring setting mid-event.

If a backup ever fails, the change is still saved, the failure is recorded in
the event log, and a red notice appears at the top of every page until it is
fixed. Usually it means the disk is full or the folder is not writable.

**After the laptop or the browser restarts,** Chrome and Edge forget their
permission to write to the folder and ask for it again. Backups pause until you
answer, and a notice at the top of every page says so: press **Reconnect** and
allow it. The app then rewrites the current files and takes a snapshot, so
nothing done while paused is missing from the folder. Your work is saved in the
browser throughout.

### If the laptop dies

1. Open `Mahjong-Tournament-System.html` on another computer.
2. Go to **Events** and use **Load a backup**.
3. Pick `restore/tournament.json` from your backup folder.

You are back exactly where the last round ended. If that folder was in OneDrive
or Google Drive, it is already on the new machine.

This is also how you move an event between computers on purpose — at lunch, or
when handing over to another organiser.

If the event is already in this browser, the app asks before replacing it, with
the time each copy was last changed. When the copy in the browser is the newer
one it says so in capitals, because loading the older backup would lose
whatever was done since.

**Keep in mind:** the browser holds the live copy, so whichever machine you carry
on from becomes the one that matters. Do not run the same event on two computers
at once and expect them to merge; they will not.
